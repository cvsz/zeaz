ZEAZ One Product Plan

A global premium business smartphone programme targeting an initial commercial launch from May 2028, followed by controlled expansion through October 2028.

Primary customer: business professionalsPremium flagship AndroidFully custom hardware with JDM partner

Planning status: All specifications, prices, budgets, dates, markets, certifications and service commitments remain planning assumptions until validated by suppliers, engineering, regulators and final commercial agreements.

1. Executive Summary

ZEAZ One is designed for executives, entrepreneurs, consultants, creators, technical professionals and other customers who depend on a phone for serious work. The five core promises are business-grade security, dependable global connectivity, long battery life and reliable performance, desktop-class mobile productivity and long-term software support.

2. Product Vision

Vision: Enable professionals to securely run work, communications, documents, meetings and AI-assisted workflows from one premium mobile device. The product promise is Your secure workday, in one phone.

3. Target Customers

The primary persona is a mobile business professional requiring secure communications, international reception, eSIM and dual-SIM flexibility, long battery endurance, dependable hotspot operation, high-quality calls, document scanning, secure file sharing, desktop functionality and fast warranty service. Secondary personas are security-conscious premium consumers and enterprise IT buyers.

4. Market Positioning

ZEAZ One occupies the territory of a secure productivity flagship for independent professionals and modern businesses. It does not attempt to win through megapixel counts, gaming-only performance, experimental foldables, the lowest price or excessive preinstalled software.

6. Hardware Product Requirements

  • Current-generation premium mobile SoC at platform freeze, selected for BSP quality, vendor support, global radio coverage, power efficiency, Android roadmap and seven-year lifecycle feasibility.
  • 12–16 GB LPDDR5X-class memory and 256–512 GB high-performance UFS storage.
  • Premium 6.6–6.8-inch LTPO OLED, adaptive 1–120 Hz, strong outdoor readability and DisplayPort over USB-C.
  • 5,200–5,600 mAh target battery, 65 W wired charging, 30 W wireless charging and configurable battery-health limits.
  • Three 50 MP-class rear cameras covering main, ultrawide and 3×–5× telephoto use, with document, whiteboard and conference-room optimisation.
  • Global 5G Sub-6, broad LTE fallback, eSIM, dual SIM, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, NFC, multi-band GNSS, USB 3.x and high-performance hotspot mode.
  • IP68 target, preferably below 225 grams and 9 mm, with service-replaceable battery, display, USB board and camera modules.

7. Software Product Plan

The device launches on the latest stable Android release supported at final platform freeze. The target lifecycle is seven years of security updates, at least six major Android upgrades, monthly security releases during the first five years and quarterly releases during the final two years.

The lightweight ZEAZ layer includes Workspace, Secure Vault, Meeting Notes, Document Scanner, Device Care, Support, Transfer, Desktop and Private AI. Desktop mode supports external monitors, resizable applications, keyboard and mouse, drag-and-drop, video meetings, browser-based office tools, remote desktop, VPN and secure storage.

8. Private AI Strategy

AI is useful, optional, understandable and privacy-controlled. Tier 1 runs on-device for rewriting, summarisation, transcription, translation, OCR, search and sensitive-data detection. Tier 2 uses organisation-approved private cloud endpoints with audit and retention controls. Tier 3 uses public cloud only with explicit user approval and clear provider/data-handling information.

9. Security Architecture

Mandatory controls include secure boot, Android Verified Boot, rollback protection, locked production bootloader, hardware-backed keys, trusted execution environment, file-based encryption, unique device identities, factory certificate injection, SELinux enforcing, signed OTA packages, anti-rollback fuses, secure reset, remote locate/wipe, software bills of materials and a vulnerability-disclosure programme.

ZEAZ directly controls root signing keys, OTA keys, provisioning certificates, device identity authorities, debug policy, recovery signing and production key ceremonies. The manufacturer must not be the exclusive key holder.

10. Enterprise Readiness

Support targets include Android work profiles, company-owned work profiles, fully managed and dedicated-device modes, Managed Google Play, zero-touch enrollment, major EMM platforms, certificate Wi-Fi, always-on and per-app VPN, remote lock/wipe, allowlists, compliance reporting and enterprise eSIM workflows.

11. Global Compliance and Certification

The programme includes Android CDD, CTS, CTS Verifier, VTS, GMS approval, Android Enterprise readiness, GCF and/or PTCRB, national radio approvals, EU CE/RED, UK requirements, GSMA TAC and IMEI allocation, Bluetooth SIG, Wi-Fi Alliance, USB-IF where applicable, battery and UN 38.3 testing, EMC, SAR, electrical safety and market-specific environmental, packaging and repairability obligations.

12. Launch Market Strategy

Wave 0, February–April 2028: 500–1,500 controlled units for staff, partners, selected businesses, researchers and certification. Wave 1, May–June 2028: Thailand, Singapore, United Kingdom, Germany, France, Netherlands, United Arab Emirates and Australia. Wave 2, July–October 2028: United States, Canada, additional Europe, Japan, South Korea, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Malaysia and Indonesia. Mainland China, India, Latin American carrier markets and government-certified secure-device markets are separate programmes.

13. Sales and Distribution

Channel sequence: direct online store, business reseller programme, enterprise pilots, selected operators and broader retail. Commercial packages cover consumer, professional and business-fleet offers, including enrollment, staging, replacement pools, extended support, asset reporting and secure disposal.

14. Customer Support and Repair

Targets include 24-hour priority response, advanced replacement in supported markets, seven-day repair completion, at least seven years of spare parts, published repair pricing, battery replacement, consent-based remote diagnostics, secure repair mode and international warranty where practical.

15. Programme Budget

Planning engineering and non-recurring expenditure is US$9.1–24.2 million. Including component inventory, manufacturing deposits, marketing, warranty reserves, logistics and service setup, recommended total programme capitalisation is approximately US$30–70 million.

17. Development Roadmap

  • Aug–Oct 2026: product definition and partner RFP.
  • Oct–Dec 2026: SoC, JDM, BSP, certification, BOM and ownership agreements.
  • Jan–Apr 2027: system, RF, mechanical, thermal, PCB, Android and factory-test architecture.
  • May–Sep 2027: EVT-A and EVT-B subsystem bring-up, cellular, camera, security and OTA.
  • Oct–Dec 2027: DVT, final design, reliability, tuning, pre-compliance and security assessment.
  • Jan–Mar 2028: certification and PVT with a mass-production gate requiring yield above 95%, stable software and completed approvals.
  • Apr–Oct 2028: controlled launch, Wave 1 and Wave 2 expansion.

18. Team Structure

The recommended internal and dedicated partner team is approximately 65–120 people across product leadership, programme management, system architecture, electrical, RF, mechanical, Android/BSP, applications, security/OTA, quality, manufacturing, supply chain, support and commercial functions.

19. Product Success Metrics

Targets include dead-on-arrival below 0.5%, 30-day returns below 3%, first-pass yield above 95%, crash-free sessions above 99.9%, critical OTA failure below 0.1%, NPS above 50, product rating above 4.3/5, support satisfaction above 90% and warranty cost below 5% of revenue.

20. Principal Risks

Major risks include insufficient SoC/BSP access, weak brand recognition, certification delays, camera quality, idle power, component shortages, lifecycle dependencies, poor factory yield, global repair complexity, excessive custom software, mishandled signing keys, simultaneous global launch and overambitious AI. Mitigation relies on binding vendor obligations, phased rollout, early compliance, experienced engineering and strict scope control.

21. Go/No-Go Decision Gates

December 2026 approves engineering only with verified platform support, contractual BSP access, an acceptable JDM proposal, funding and viable COGS. April 2027 releases the first PCB only after RF, thermal, mechanical, power, component and driver approval. September 2027 proceeds to DVT only when core systems work and battery safety passes. March 2028 approves mass production only after certification, factory yield, final OTA, service readiness and closure of critical defects.

22. First 90-Day Execution Plan

Days 1–30: appoint ownership, approve concept and budget, create the PRD, define launch markets and bands, begin customer research, recruit the hardware architect and open platform/JDM discussions. Days 31–60: issue the RFP, evaluate SoCs and BSP support, build the BOM and financial model, create design concepts, define software, OTA, cloud, warranty and certification architecture. Days 61–90: select platform and JDM, negotiate source/IP ownership, freeze initial dimensions and component classes, approve EVT scheduling, establish the risk register and make the first formal gate decision.

23. Final Recommendation

Proceed only as a premium business flagship using one global platform where practical and an experienced JDM while ZEAZ retains control of source, schematics, PCB files, factory tools, signing keys and certification records. Commit to long-term support before SoC selection, launch in controlled waves, prioritise security, endurance, connectivity, desktop productivity and service, avoid unnecessary generation-one complexity and capitalise the complete programme rather than engineering alone.

Recommended commercial target: May 2028 initial launch, expansion through October 2028, US$30–70 million programme capital and 100,000–200,000 first-year devices.
